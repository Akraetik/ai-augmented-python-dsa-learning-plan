## The idea

Some answers at index `i` depend on **everything to the left** and **everything to the right**.
Computing both for every `i` from scratch is O(n²).

Instead, do two sweeps.

1. Sweep left to right. Store what you need about the left side at each index.
2. Sweep right to left. Store what you need about the right side.
3. Combine.

Each sweep is O(n). Total O(n).

You met the left half of this in Section 00: running sum. That's a prefix sum. Now you'll use prefixes of other
things: products, maximums, and rule-checks.

## Worked example: biggest number to the left of each position

Not a lab. Same idea.

```python
def max_to_left(nums):
    out = [0] * len(nums)
    best = float("-inf")
    for i in range(len(nums)):
        out[i] = best              # everything strictly LEFT of i
        best = max(best, nums[i])  # now include i, for the next one
    return out

max_to_left([3, 1, 4, 1, 5])  # [-inf, 3, 3, 4, 4]
```

Look at the order: **record first, then include yourself.** That gives "strictly left of i."
Swap the two lines and you get "left of i, including i." Both are useful. Know which you need.

## Product of Array Except Self (lab 13)

For each `i`, return the product of every other number. **No division.**

Split "every other number" into two parts:

```
answer[i] = (product of everything LEFT of i) × (product of everything RIGHT of i)
```

```
nums:          [2,  3,  4,  5]
left product:  [1,  2,  6, 24]   ← empty product is 1
right product: [60, 20, 5,  1]
answer:        [60, 40, 30, 24]
```

That's O(n) time with two extra lists. The follow-up asks for O(1) extra space (the output doesn't count).
Hint for later: you can store the left products directly in the output list, then sweep right to left with a
single running variable.

Why no division? Zeros. Divide by zero crashes, and "total ÷ nums[i]" can't handle a zero in the list.
Test your code on `[1, 0, 3]` and `[0, 0, 2]`.

## Trapping Rain Water (lab 14, Stretch)

Bars of different heights. Rain falls. How much water sits between them?

Focus on **one** position. How high does water rise above it?

```
 level 4:          █
 level 3:      █ ~ █
 level 2:  █ ~ █ ~ █ █
 level 1:  █ ~ █ █ █ █
           0 1 2 3 4 5     heights [2, 0, 3, 1, 4, 2]  →  4 units of water (~)
```

Water at position `i` is limited by the **shorter** of: the tallest bar to its left, and the tallest bar to its right.
Water above `i` = that limit minus the bar's own height (never below 0).

```
water[i] = min(max_left[i], max_right[i]) - height[i]
```

Now you know what to precompute. Two sweeps. Then one combine. That's the O(n) time, O(n) space answer.

There's a two-pointer O(1)-space version too. You'll revisit this problem in Section 02.

## Candy (lab 15, Stretch)

Kids stand in a line with ratings. Each kid gets at least one candy. A kid with a higher rating than a
**neighbor** must get more candy than that neighbor. Find the minimum total.

Two rules: one about the left neighbor, one about the right neighbor. Handle them separately.

1. Sweep left to right. Satisfy only "more than my left neighbor if I'm rated higher."
2. Sweep right to left. Satisfy "more than my right neighbor if I'm rated higher" — **without breaking rule 1.**

The careful part is step 2. If a kid already has enough from step 1, don't lower it. Think about what
`max(...)` does for you there.

## Common mistakes

- **Off-by-one in the prefix:** including `nums[i]` in its own "left product." Check the order of record vs. include.
- **Starting product at 0** instead of 1. Anything times 0 is 0.
- **Trapping water:** forgetting that an edge position can't hold water. The formula handles it, if your max arrays include the bar itself.
- **Candy:** in the second sweep, overwriting instead of taking the max. Breaks rule 1.

## Pattern triggers

> **"For each i, something about everything else" → prefix pass + suffix pass, then combine.**
> **"Water / area limited by the shorter side" → min(max_left, max_right).**
> **"Two neighbor rules" → one sweep per direction, merge with max.**
