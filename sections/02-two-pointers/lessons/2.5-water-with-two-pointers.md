## Where we left it

In lesson 1.6 you solved Trapping Rain Water with two sweeps:

```
water[i] = min(max_left[i], max_right[i]) - height[i]
```

That needed two extra lists: O(n) space. Now drop them.

## The key insight

You don't need **both** maxes exactly. You only need the **smaller** one. That's what sets the water level.

Run two pointers from the ends. Keep `left_max` (tallest seen from the left) and `right_max` (tallest seen
from the right).

Suppose `left_max < right_max`. Look at position `left`:

- Its true max on the left is `left_max`. That's exact.
- Its true max on the right is **at least** `right_max`, because some bar on the right is already that tall.
- So the smaller of the two is `left_max`. We know the water level at `left` for sure.

```
water at left = left_max - height[left]
```

Then move `left` inward. The mirror case handles `right`.

**Process the side with the smaller max.** That side's water level is already decided.

## Trace it

`height = [2, 0, 3, 1, 4, 2]` (the picture from lesson 1.6, answer 4).

| step | left | right | left_max | right_max | which side | water added | total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | 5 | 2 | 2 | right (tie → pick one rule and stick to it) | 2 − 2 = 0 | 0 |
| 2 | 0 | 4 | 2 | 4 | left | 2 − 2 = 0 | 0 |
| 3 | 1 | 4 | 2 | 4 | left | 2 − 0 = 2 | 2 |
| 4 | 2 | 4 | 3 | 4 | left | 3 − 3 = 0 | 2 |
| 5 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 4 | left | 3 − 1 = 2 | 4 |
| 6 | 4 | 4 | — | — | stop | | **4** |

(Here each pointer's max is updated with its own bar **before** computing water, so water is never negative.)
