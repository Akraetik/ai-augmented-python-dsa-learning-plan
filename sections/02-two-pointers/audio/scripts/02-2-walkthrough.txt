# 02-2 Two Pointers Walkthrough

N: Section two, track two. The walkthrough. Answer out loud before the answer voice.
[pause 1]
N: Valid Palindrome. Ignore anything that isn't a letter or digit. Ignore case. Does it read the same both ways?
N: What do you compare at each step?
[pause 3]
A: The character at left and the character at right, both lowercased.
N: And when the character at left is a comma?
[pause 3]
A: Skip it. Move left forward until it's on a letter or digit.
N: That skip loop needs a second check. What is it?
[pause 4]
A: Left is less than right. Otherwise a string of only punctuation runs off the end.
[pause 1]
N: Two Sum two. Sorted list. Find the pair that adds to the target. Return positions starting from one.
N: Numbers are one, three, four, eight. Target is seven. Start at the ends. One plus eight is nine. What moves?
[pause 4]
A: Right moves. Nine is too big. Only a smaller right number can bring it down.
N: Now one plus four is five. What moves?
[pause 3]
A: Left moves. Five is too small.
N: Three plus four is seven. What do you return?
[pause 4]
A: Two and three. The positions, counted from one.
N: Say the reason it's safe to drop the left number when the sum is too small.
[pause 6]
A: The right number is the biggest partner left. If even it isn't enough, the left number has no partner.
[pause 1]
N: Squares of a Sorted Array. Negative four, negative one, zero, three, ten. Return the squares, sorted.
N: Where are the biggest squares?
[pause 4]
A: At the two ends. The most negative and the most positive.
N: So where does each big square go?
[pause 3]
A: Into the last open slot of the answer. Fill from the back.
[pause 1]
N: Is Subsequence. Is a c e inside a b c d e, in order?
N: Which pointer moves every step?
[pause 3]
A: The one in the longer string, t.
N: When does the other one move?
[pause 3]
A: Only on a match.
N: When do you return true?
[pause 3]
A: When the pointer in s reaches the end of s.
[pause 1]
N: Container With Most Water. What's the area of two lines?
[pause 4]
A: The width between them, times the shorter line.
N: The left line is three tall. The right line is ten tall. Which one moves?
[pause 3]
A: The left. The shorter one.
N: Why?
[pause 6]
A: Every container that keeps the short line is narrower and no taller. None can beat the one you just measured.
[pause 1]
N: Three Sum. Find every unique triple that sums to zero.
N: First step?
[pause 3]
A: Sort the list.
N: Then?
[pause 5]
A: For each index i, fix that number, and run Two Sum two on the rest, looking for minus that number.
N: Big O?
[pause 3]
A: O of n squared.
N: Name the three duplicate skips.
[pause 8]
A: Skip a fixed number that equals the one before it. After a match, move left past repeats. And move right past repeats.
N: For the fixed number, why compare with the one before, and not the one after?
[pause 6]
A: Comparing with the one after skips the first copy. Then a triple like minus one, minus one, two is lost.
N: The fixed number is positive. What now?
[pause 3]
A: Break. Everything after it is positive too.
[pause 1]
N: Three Sum Closest. What changes?
[pause 5]
A: Track the sum closest to the target. Return right away on an exact hit. No duplicate skipping.
[pause 1]
N: Trapping Rain Water with two pointers. Left max is three. Right max is seven. Which side is ready?
[pause 4]
A: The left side. Its level is three for sure, because the right already has a taller bar.
N: What did this version save over section one's?
[pause 3]
A: Space. No extra lists. O of one.
[pause 1]
N: Last one. A new problem. Sorted list. Count the pairs whose sum is below k. Which shape?
[pause 5]
A: Opposite ends. When the pair at left and right is below k, every pair from left to anything up to right works. Add right minus left, then move left.
